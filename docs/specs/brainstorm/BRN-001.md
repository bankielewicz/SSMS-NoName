# BRN-001 — NoName: an SSMS 22 suite of DBA tools around a redacted AI prompt builder

## 1. Context

**Version 2 (2026-10-31): one instance, not an estate.** At the design step Bryan redirected the suite: "the mockups depict more of an enterprise monitoring platform. i'm looking for the extensions to work on a single instaace leveraging the latest capabilities of sql server such as query store, extended events, sql error log, perfmon counters, dynamic management views - perhaps have the ability to capture information historically in a local database to extend the ssms extension.... it appears that the dashboard has many links and with a ssms extension hosted on a pc it can cause allot of lag. We need to make the SSMS extension build look amazing with as many bells and whistels as possible." History lives on the instance, in a small database filled by Agent jobs (his choice), and "noname is not yet the product name. it's presently the &lt;slug&gt; as we don't have an accurate name that represents the suite of ssms extensions being developed". He asked for the design to iterate until it is impressive. Research of 2026-10-31 (single-instance SQL Server tools, SQL Server 2022 and 2025 features, the Visual Studio SDK's performance guidance; repositories in `tmp/` read for ideas only, never copied) informs IDEA-38 to IDEA-49. Version 1's text below still holds where version 2 doesn't change it.

Bryan wants an SSMS 22 extension that builds AI prompts locally from SQL Server context and redacts sensitive information first, for organizations that don't let AI tools connect to databases directly (INPUT-NN-003). It must work whether or not SSMS's Copilot/AI component is installed (INPUT-NN-009); MCP is optional. It is for Bryan first, then open source (INPUT-NN-007).

The first version of this draft held only the prompt builder. Bryan, 2026-10-30: "While this looks good, this is by no means what I envision as the SSMS Extension suite we're looking to build as MVP." He then brought in a second session's work (handoff of 2026-10-31): 16 tool ideas from his own list of reference products (B8 below), a shared audit rule (S4) and a 20-board Claude Design canvas. His decisions on 2026-10-31:
- One brainstorm for the whole suite. The prompt builder is the hub: nine tools hand evidence to it through "Build prompt…" (the handoff says six but lists nine).
- `devforgeai:ui` runs before the PRD and imports boards from the existing canvas.
- "NoName owns the entire lifecycle of ssms extension from design through to the backend code. use learn.microsoft.com and the local instance for development purposes." Server-side components are NoName's own, separate from the in-process extension. Third-party repositories in the ignored `tmp/` folder are for research only, never copied.
- Audit (S4) is in the MVP: NoName ships the audit installer. Without the server's audit table, kill, capture start and stop, decrypt and data search refuse and offer that installer. Safe-set job delegation is a requirement after the MVP ("option 1 & 2 are for post-mvp", then "include audit capability").

Bryan's words from the second session, all 2026-10-30, recorded as B1 to B9 in the handoff's section 2: B2, the quality bar: mockups must be "profession or enterprise grade mockups with \"bells and whistles\" that I'm looking for in a SSMS extension while also being truly effective"; B3, "will also need to design the data collection portion for these types of designs"; B4 and B5, one server and many servers, with a scope switch ("Yes"); B8, the list of reference products quoted in IDEA-22 to IDEA-31; B9, "auditing these events will become important in the development of the ssms extensions." B10, the limit on job delegation, is a side agent's note that Bryan relayed, not his words.

**Classes.** Every capability is classed:
- **A:** inside SSMS, proven. Only P1 today: the VSIX loads and its Tools-menu and query-window commands fire (ASM-02).
- **B:** inside SSMS, waiting on a named probe. Recorded as an assumption whose validation is the probe; probes proposed in the SQLServer-BP docs join RES-NN-001 only if Bryan agrees.
- **C:** outside SSMS: server-side objects (audit table, JobOps package, collectors, XE sessions), a central repository, CI, git. Now NoName-owned, but never part of the in-process extension; every extension feature says what still works without them.

The mockups show example data from a fictional estate and are not evidence that anything works. Their web-style frame is a mockup shell; in SSMS each tool is a tool window, dialog, menu command or info bar.

This BRN carries the earlier brainstorm IDEA-NN-001 v0.3.0 and the feasibility spike brief RES-NN-001 into the DevForgeAI planning chain; both stay in `docs/specs/noname/`. Owner inputs, evidence (E-01 to E-20), constraints (C-01 to C-08) and open questions (Q-NN-*) keep their IDs there and are cited here, not restated. The next step after this BRN is `devforgeai:ui`, then the PRD.

## 3. Target users

| Persona | Job to be done | Current workaround | Pain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bryan, the owner and first user (DBA and developer) | Get AI help with a query, plan, error or deadlock at work | Redact by hand, or don't use AI | Slow and leak-prone (hypothesis E-09); no AI inside SSMS where Copilot is off |
| Open-source adopters (proposed, not observed) | The same job in their own organizations | The same | The same; plus each has different sensitivity rules (PRB-06) |
| Security and compliance reviewers (affected, not users) | Decide whether the tool is acceptable at work | Ban the tool or trust it blindly | Need to see what leaves the machine and what isn't guaranteed (PRB-04) |
| The maintainer (Bryan, later contributors) | Keep the extension working across frequent SSMS updates | Wait for breakage reports | Undocumented APIs change between releases (PRB-09) |
| DBA in an incident or a change window | See live waits, blocking, replicas and jobs; run change scripts safely | Activity Monitor, Job Activity Monitor, one-shot procedures, hand-edited scripts | One-instance views, no history, unchecked kills and apply-mode runs (PRB-11 to PRB-23) |
| DBA with a multi-server estate (AGs and DAGs) | Health, backups, patching and security across many servers | Per-server checks and spreadsheets | No estate view; history lost when SSMS closes (PRB-28, PRB-29) |
| Job operator in an AD group (not sysadmin) | Start, stop or re-time specific Agent jobs | Ask a DBA | Waiting, or rights that amount to sysadmin (PRB-24) |

Users whose organizations allow Copilot with MCP are served by Copilot today; an optional MCP channel could serve them later (IDEA-06). macOS and Linux users are excluded by the host: SSMS runs only on Windows.

## 5. Evaluation method

**Version 2.** IDEA-38 to IDEA-49 are rated with the same rule as version 1 (below). IDEA-38 and IDEA-39 lead with Bryan's words; the shape after them, and IDEA-40 to IDEA-49, are Claude's, from the research of 2026-10-31. IDEA-42 is high risk because it depends on handing plans to SSMS's own viewer or an out-of-process one (ASM-28). IDEA-38 and IDEA-39 score only 1 because they are large, but they carry Bryan's direction: one instance, light on the PC, and history on the instance.

Diverge-converge: problems were framed first, ideas were generated without judgment, and each idea was then rated high, medium or low for value, effort and risk. The score is 2 × value − effort − risk, counting high = 3, medium = 2 and low = 1 (range −4 to 4); a higher score is a stronger candidate for promotion.

**One rating rule for all 37 ideas** (proposed in the SQLServer-BP handoff; Bryan confirms or corrects it at convergence):
- **Effort** comes from the spec's Size where one exists: S is low, S-M and M are medium, L is high. Ideas without a spec are judged the same way.
- **Risk** is high when the idea needs the execute intercept (P2), the per-window badge (P8), editor squiggles as a requirement (PRB-ED-05), any results-grid probe (PRB-GRID-*) or the JobOps spike (PRB-JC-01), or carries a named non-probe risk (IDEA-06's MCP policy, E-03; IDEA-09's placeholder survival, ASM-08). Otherwise medium. No idea is low risk while only P1 is validated, so IDEA-02, IDEA-04, IDEA-05 and IDEA-10 to IDEA-14 moved from low to medium since the first draft.
- **Value** is Bryan's call: how often the pain hits him and how much damage it does. The values here are Claude's proposals, from the specs' own ranking ("used on nearly every script run", "used daily", "rare") and Bryan's inputs.

Attribution: IDEA-01 to IDEA-06 are Bryan's, in his words from IDEA-NN-001 and RES-NN-001; where a sentence of explanation follows his words, it is Claude's. IDEA-07 to IDEA-15 are Claude's. IDEA-16 to IDEA-21 are Claude's ideas from the SQLServer-BP session. In IDEA-22 to IDEA-31 the leading item and URL are Bryan's (B8) and the spec is Claude's; IDEA-29's core is Bryan's sentence. IDEA-32 and IDEA-35 lead with Bryan's sentences (B9, B3). IDEA-33, IDEA-34, IDEA-36 and IDEA-37 come from canvas boards Claude designed; IDEA-33's scope switch is Bryan's "Yes" (B5).
